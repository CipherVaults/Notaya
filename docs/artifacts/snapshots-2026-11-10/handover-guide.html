📐 PBS, the method

PBS is the AI Project Bootstrap System. You adopted its smallest useful form for Notaya yourself on 2026-10-27 19:08:33 UTC (A29). On 2026-11-07 21:56:54 UTC Ahmed made it the method for all Notaya development on his side, "across the entire development of Notaya, not just this task". The skill is in .claude/skills/pbs-start-resume-handoff/ (method version 1.1). Ahmed's side

The work cycle for every task

  1. Start. Read the partner's latest instruction, STATUS and the amendments record. Look at the live repository. Take the baseline commit from git log.
  2. State the task. Baseline commit, the partner's message with its UTC time, exact scope and write area, what will be returned, the stop point. If one is missing, ask or take the narrowest reading and say which.
  3. Keep three things apart: permission, observation, acceptance (above).
  4. Name the result state of what you hand back: produced (it exists), verified (named checks passed on this exact source), integrated (merged into main), accepted (a partner said so). Most results stop at verified.
  5. Stop at the approved endpoint. "Standing by" is not completion. Say what is done, what is unverified and what you wait for.
  6. Record by size. Small: the PR description. Normal: an Execution Summary plus its index row, the changed lines of STATUS. Big: the 12-section Execution Summary and a Session Summary entry.

What Ahmed added on top

  • Thorough, mandatory documentation. Records are read in full before acting, never skimmed. A model does not know the project's context unless it has read it (Ahmed, 20:12:10). Ahmed's side
  • Every session that changes the repository, records a decision or ends a work block gets a Session Summary entry in the full 13-section form, with the "Models, Effort, Roles and Provenance" block. No exemption for small tasks on his side. Ahmed's side
  • One session file. docs/sessions.md holds all entries, newest on top, oldest at the bottom, "even if it takes thousands of lines". Older entries are never rewritten; a correction is a dated note. Ahmed's side
  • This guide is a living document. It is updated at every milestone or decision, in both copies, with a change log (A39, Ahmed 22:33:37). Ahmed's side
  • The Notion hub (roadmap and feature board) and the PBS records are updated at each milestone, written so Mohamed can see what was done. The hub is private in Ahmed's Notion; the repository records win where they differ. Ahmed's side

🔌 The AI CLI foundation

Ahmed uses Claude and Codex from outside to drive Notaya locally, in the way Claude uses Notion through MCP, but with more power and freedom, "because it's local and it's our code and it's our app". Ahmed's side

Agreed in principle

  • Every part of Notaya is reachable. The AI sees everything; locked notes only after they are unlocked.
  • The AI may create, edit and delete. Deletes need confirmation.
  • An activity log records everything the AI does.
  • Every feature is designed with named AI actions from its first design, including moving and copying pages and databases between places.
  • Long-term north star (direction only, not now): Notaya eventually matches Notion closely enough that Ahmed's whole Notion can be pulled in as-is, as an always-on local backup.

Safety direction, agreed 22:45:28 and 22:49:30 UTC (A41)

Direction only: nothing is built and no promise is in docs/security-design.md yet. Ahmed's reason: he fears the attack surface of an AI that can delete, write, change or even make malware, and also does not want a weaker feature set or a home-made security system ("We are not cybersecurity experts, but we have to do our best").

  • Security is part of every feature from its first design.
  • Sealed in: the AI reaches only Notaya's notes; no programs, no other files, no internet.
  • Off by default; the on/off switch lives only in Notaya's own Settings, so the AI cannot flip it.
  • Permission levels (read-only, can edit, full) and limits to chosen notebooks; deletes go to a recycle bin after the person confirms; AI edits are kept in version history and can be undone; a log of AI actions that the AI cannot edit; locked notes stay locked.
  • Proven, well-known patterns; one simple confirm for deletes and very large changes; the AI is never given a way to change its own permissions; backups are the safety net.

Not decided yet

  • The action list itself (a thread is collecting it for Ahmed to review).
  • How an outside program reaches Notaya and proves it is allowed.
  • Whether the AI may work while the Notaya window is open, and how the window refreshes after an outside change (today the interface keeps its own copy of the data and is not refreshed from outside).
  • Where a delete is confirmed, where the activity log lives, and how it is made tamper-proof.

Security. The AI CLI creates a new trust boundary. Under CLAUDE.md, changing security behavior needs the partner's own words, and docs/security-design.md must be updated with any new promise. Keys and secrets stay in the Rust core. Because this touches shared code and shared promises, Ahmed's design should be shown to Mohamed before it is built, and each promise must be written into docs/security-design.md, tested and reviewed first.
