# 🔧 Maintain without drift

## 🎯 Why updates fail

A document can become more complete while becoming less useful. A common failure is to recover important new context and copy it into every file. Architecture becomes a current-status page; Roadmap becomes a task list; Methodology accumulates temporary examples; Session history becomes a migration receipt.

The remedy is clear ownership and controlled change, not indiscriminate shortening. Several documents may legitimately change after one event when different owned answers changed. Review each against its purpose; do not impose a blanket one-file-only rule.

## ⚖️ An update and a contract change are different decisions

Permission to update a document's facts does not automatically authorize changing the job that document performs. Read its actual accepted purpose, audience, owned questions, depth, expected rate of change, exclusions and update trigger. A familiar filename is insufficient: Current State may be a live checkpoint in one project and durable phase orientation in another.

Existing boundaries can be imperfect. If the operating system has evolved, propose the needed ownership change explicitly and preserve the prior contract's history. Do not pretend the older document was already perfectly separated or silently adopt a new role while “refreshing” its content.

A well-written ownership table does not establish that the document obeys it. Test representative actual passages against that table. A true, carefully dated or qualified fact can still be misplaced. Ask why the receiving document needs it. Conversely, a numerical gate may be a deliberately accepted planning constraint. Do not remove or generalize it merely because numbers can age; establish its authority, purpose and appropriate owner first.

## 🔎 Notice drift at meaningful work boundaries

At a substantive stop, amendment, handoff or re-entry, compare the affected record with its adopted purpose/profile and any later approved change. Look for lost decision/evidence/unfinished-work retrievability, stale “latest” indexes, misleading date coverage and current instructions contradicting their owner—not merely heading counts. Do not scan or reformat the whole archive after every small action.

When bounded maintenance is authorized, correct affected current pointers and record structure while preserving original facts, source links and chronology; use a dated addendum/reconciled view where rewriting would imply knowledge that did not exist. Missing times or decisions stay unknown. If a format-policy change or write is outside authority, propose the precise repair instead. Respect a legitimate new profile, and keep a tiny update compact. Read back the repair and check that a fresh reader can find the effective decision, evidence limit and next action.

## 🧭 Before editing

1. Confirm the actual request, destination and permitted scope.
2. Read the complete target when practical. If it is too large, inspect its index, relevant sections and boundaries; say what remains unread.
3. State the target's purpose in one sentence.
4. Identify the facts that changed and their canonical owners.
5. Find existing accepted wording, useful history, links, protected content and other collaborators' work.
6. Decide whether a targeted edit is enough. A broad rewrite requires a concrete reason and the appropriate authority.
7. Preserve a recoverable baseline when the risk of loss warrants it.

A matching filename does not prove two copies contain the same revision. Compare content, source identity and dates.

## 🗺️ Use an owner-impact map

For each meaningful change, record:

| Changed fact or decision | Evidence and decision status | Owning source | Intended treatment | Justified secondary representation | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Actual changed claim] | [Observed / reported / proposed / accepted, with source] | [Canonical owner] | [Retain / update / cross-reference / relocate / preserve as history / ask / no change] | [A brief dated snapshot, link or index when its reader needs it] | [Meaning and destination check] |

Only affected owners belong in the update set. “Unchanged, because its owned facts did not change” is a valid result.

## 🛠️ The document-by-document procedure

### 1. Restate purpose
For example: “This Architecture explains enduring components and boundaries.” That immediately exposes current task details that belong elsewhere.

### 2. Preserve the core
Keep useful explanations, chronology, rationale, accepted decisions and references. Do not flatten depth because a shorter output is easier to generate.

When removing or moving meaningful detail, name where its necessary meaning survives and check that location. A long Session archive does not prove it already contains the exact source, exception, time boundary or continuation detail being removed. Use a small preservation ledger when change is substantial:

| Material being changed | Why it matters | Keep / correct / relocate / deliberately remove | Surviving location or owner decision | Checked result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Claim, requirement, exception, rationale, link or historical passage] | [Future use] | [Treatment] | [Exact location or explicit decision] | [Verified / gap] |

### 3. Make the smallest coherent change
Change the sections whose facts changed. If related sections need synchronization, list them explicitly. Do not copy a whole source into every owner.

### 4. Compare meaning before and after
For an authority change such as pause, supersession or resumed permission, inspect the affected entry route and all current-facing executable instructions. A new banner at the top can coexist with a contradictory “continue” instruction lower down. Update the owning rule and its dependent pointers; preserve the earlier approved scope as dated history rather than erasing it.

Check:
- facts retained, corrected or removed;
- reasons and alternatives retained;
- historical dates preserved;
- authority unchanged unless explicitly amended;
- live state separated from proposals;
- document identity and writing method preserved;
- no unsupported identity, timing, provenance or success labels introduced.

A line count is a useful warning signal, not a semantic quality test. A repair can become longer while improving ownership. Conversely, a well-structured replacement is not automatically a semantic superset of the rejected draft. Compare meaningful deletions, generalized details and newly absolute wording; report any unverified preservation gap.

### 5. Save and verify
Keep four independent judgments visible:
1. **Integrity:** Did the intended source bytes/records and required history survive?
2. **Semantic fitness:** Does each candidate still serve its contract and preserve needed meaning?
3. **Publication:** Was the intended version saved to and read back from the actual destination?
4. **Acceptance:** Did the appropriate reviewer/owner accept the particular claim or document?

A rejected revision can preserve all old bytes and still fail semantic fitness. A correct header/readable file does not prove a full semantic regression check. A receipt describing a prepared bundle cannot accept its content. A permission error during publication is separate from a writing/ownership defect; successful publication would not cure that defect.

Distinguish:
- prepared locally;
- write attempted;
- write succeeded;
- destination read back;
- rendered appearance checked;
- owner accepted.

Verify the actual destination rather than the local draft alone. A failed or uncertain write needs inspection before retry, especially when repetition could duplicate content.

### 6. Update navigation and report
Repair affected links/indexes. State what changed, what remained unchanged by design, what was verified and what is pending. Avoid claiming all sources synchronized after checking only one copy.

## 🔗 Purposeful repetition without competing owners

One primary owner does not mean one mention anywhere. Architecture can name and link a phase model; Current can record that a phase began; Roadmap owns the phase definition and gates. Session history can preserve a dated operational snapshot after the live source changes. Failure Intelligence can retain precise measurements needed to explain an incident while Methodology holds the adopted general prevention rule.

A secondary mention should serve its reader, identify its source and date/scope, and avoid claiming independent live authority. The problem is several separately editable records governing the same changing truth, not all repetition.

## 📘 Historical summaries

Keep prior dated bodies intact unless correcting a demonstrable factual/formatting error under the project's change rule. A later correction should identify what was corrected and why; it should not make later knowledge appear to have existed earlier.

For cumulative Session Summary archives, use newest-first entries and a small useful index. When splitting into dated files, preserve ordering and stable references. Do not insert release-packaging banners into the middle of historical session bodies.

A malformed new prepend can be replaced from a verified clean base when authorized. Preserve the original evidence separately when needed to understand the incident; do not keep a defective narrative merely to avoid admitting the correction.

## 🧳 Controlled migrations

A methodology refresh does not automatically authorize migrating existing projects.

A deliberate migration needs:
- the problem it solves;
- scope and exclusions;
- old owner → new owner mapping;
- protected content and access boundaries;
- preservation/rollback proportionate to consequence;
- change sequence;
- verification and acceptance;
- exact continuation if interrupted.

Make the migration recoverable before broad edits. Do not begin by erasing the old structure and hoping a complete replacement will be finished before interruption.

## 📍 Live truth versus durable design

A live database may change every day. Architecture may change rarely. Current State should describe the accepted present at the scale assigned by that project, not mirror every operational field.

Keep fast-changing records in their real operational source. Refer to them from durable orientation. This avoids maintaining the same volatile fact in several places.

## ⚠️ If sources disagree

Do not choose by timestamp alone.
1. Identify the question and its owner.
2. Locate the actual authorization if permission is at issue.
3. Inspect live artifacts/evidence if observation is at issue.
4. Check requirement and decision history if acceptance is at issue.
5. Preserve the contradiction and any uncertain gap.
6. Correct only the affected owners after reconciliation.

## ✅ Before declaring the update complete

- [ ] The target's purpose survived.
- [ ] Historical context and useful depth were preserved.
- [ ] Only affected owners changed.
- [ ] Approval and acceptance were not invented.
- [ ] No private data leaked into a portable example.
- [ ] Saved content and links were read back.
- [ ] Untested rendering/behavior is stated.
- [ ] Current, Continuation and indexes agree where their facts were affected.
- [ ] Pending work has an owner and exact next action.

## 🌱 Adopting a new method

Observe a concrete problem, propose a bounded change, try it proportionally, review the result and record adoption. Keep project-specific workarounds local until evidence supports broader use.

A polished template is not proof that the method has been validated.
