## 🔄 Core learning and refinement cycle

### 1. Construct

Form the smallest useful candidate, hypothesis, or approach from the available evidence. State what it is intended to achieve and the assumptions it depends on. Do not silently turn an exploratory idea into a requirement.

### 2. Test

Choose a check appropriate to the claim and consequence. Specify the inputs, artifact/configuration version, expected outcome, and known limitations. Preserve enough evidence for another reader to understand the result.

### 3. Compare

Compare the result with the baseline, acceptance conditions, and meaningful alternatives. Separate an observed improvement from a preference or an untested explanation. Do not keep experimenting merely because more alternatives exist.

### 4. Apply human judgment

Bring consequential tradeoffs, scope choices, and acceptance decisions to the actual decision-maker. An AI recommendation, passing check, or completed draft is not automatically an owner decision.

### 5. Refine under control

Change the smallest coherent part that addresses the finding. Preserve useful work and explain material tradeoffs. If the approved outcome, risk, permissions, cost, or external commitment changes, obtain the required amendment before the dependent action.

### 6. Temporarily lock

When the agreed standard is met, stop refining that part. Record the accepted baseline and what evidence supports it. Reopen it only for an actual defect, changed requirement, invalidated assumption, or a newly authorized improvement.

## 🗺️ From intent to authorized work

For substantial new work:

1. Inspect existing reality and clarify the intended benefit, boundaries, and unknowns.
2. Produce a proposed Plan with alternatives, risks, checks, and stopping conditions.
3. Review it; record amendments and unresolved decisions.
4. Establish the approved bounded Goal and actual approval reference.
5. Implement with an adaptive Plan inside that Goal.
6. Reconcile artifacts, evidence, and affected records; distinguish technical completion from owner acceptance.
7. Stop at the agreed boundary. A successor requires its own authority.

A small clear task may use one bounded Task Brief that records the approach, actual authority, and checks. Do not force a full project ceremony onto an isolated task.

## 🧾 Evidence practice

For a consequential claim, record what was observed, where and when, which input/artifact/configuration it applies to, and what it does not establish. Label owner reports, indirect evidence, inference, and unverified claims accurately.

A historical successful check remains history. Reassess its applicability after material changes. Evidence freshness and the historical outcome are separate: an old valid observation can become insufficient for a current decision without becoming false.

## ⚖️ Decisions and disagreements

Describe the question, alternatives, relevant evidence, and material tradeoffs before recording a consequential choice. Name the actual decision-maker and source of approval. Preserve why a reasonable alternative was rejected.

When contributors disagree, isolate the disputed claim or criterion and obtain the smallest useful additional evidence. Use independent critique where the consequence or uncertainty earns it. Do not settle a disagreement by duplicating conflicting conclusions across documents.

## 👥 Collaboration and handoffs

Give each worker a bounded outcome, relevant context, source owners, allowed targets, constraints, acceptance checks, and a return destination. Do not assign overlapping edits without an integration arrangement.

A worker returns artifacts, observed changes, evidence, assumptions, unresolved issues, and any source/permission conflict. The parent or integrating owner reviews the combined result and affected interfaces before whole-system acceptance. A collection of worker successes is not sufficient integration evidence.

## 📚 Controlled documentation maintenance

1. Identify the changed fact or decision and the question it affects.
2. Consult the source map and list only the genuinely affected owners.
3. Read one owner fully enough to understand its contract and existing useful content.
4. Restate that document's purpose and preserve a recoverable baseline when warranted.
5. Make a scoped change, preserving identity, useful depth, and historical truth.
6. Inspect the diff and read back the intended destination before claiming it updated.
7. Continue to the next affected owner only after the current one is sound.

Do not refresh every document merely because new context exists. Architecture owns durable structure; Roadmap owns phase progression; Methodology owns recurring practice. Live operational detail belongs in its assigned operational owner.

A substantive Session Summary preserves chronology, reasoning, alternatives, friction, corrections, and re-entry context. Use the full established template. Keep new entries newest-first, preserve older dated truth, and do not replace the archive with a migration report or abbreviated checkpoint.

## 🧷 Interruption and recovery

Inspect live artifacts and active operations before replaying steps. Compare them with the last accepted state and continuation record. Classify useful work as accepted/current, complete but unreconciled, unverified, partial, or unavailable. Preserve it and do only the missing verification, reconciliation, or implementation.

Never assume a write succeeded because it was attempted, or that a task failed because its summary is missing. Distinguish prepared, write attempted, saved, destination verified, technically accepted, and owner accepted.
